Building Tomorrow's Operations Managers

Operations is the discipline the folklore misreads in two directions at once: the clerk's version (the counter of units, the filler of logs, the chaser of status) and the hero's version (the expeditor, the all-nighter, the rescue artist whose operation produces instability dressed as dedication). This structural guide treats the operations manager as the keeper and interpreter of the conversion system - the flow's anatomy (throughput, dependability, cost, safety, ranked in writing); the pulse's legibility (the lexicon, the pairing, the cadence, the tail's reading); the bottleneck's discipline (exploit before you subordinate, subordinate before you elevate); the queue's law (the utilization's trap, the work-in-flight's gate, the buffer's deliberate placement); the failure's anatomy (the ledger, the maintenance portfolio, the rehearsed restart); the variance's two causes (the band's honesty and the tampering the protocol refuses); the schedule as a contract priced at the constraint's arithmetic; the flow's geography (the walls, the inventory's placement, the handoff's seam); the machine in the loop (the algorithm's audit, the twin's honesty, the alerts' portfolio); and the five-rhythm operating program that lets the flow survive its keeper's absence. The machine era has absorbed the operations mechanics faster than any craft in this series - and concentrated the reading that remains: the operations manager as the system's interpreter, who knows what the numbers mean and stands behind what they promise. The thirteenth volume of Building Tomorrow's People.
