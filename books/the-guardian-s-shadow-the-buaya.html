Introduction There is a river in every volume of this series, but only in this one does the river have a lord. The buaya — the crocodile of the Malay waters — is the register's richest animal-file, and the tradition read him not as a beast among beasts but as a sovereign of the water: a being with a species-register of his own, a doctrine of named fangs that decides which men die, a legal oath he swears to the witnesses of heaven, a covenant with the pawangs that runs exactly as far as the river's reaches, and a place in the birth-cosmology as the placenta's twin — the water-sibling of the forest's tiger.
