Jihad: The Striving in the Cause

$0.99 · DRM-free EPUB + PDF

Jihad: The Striving in the Cause reads the Quran's most famous and least-read word. The verb before the politics: striving as pressure (29:8), as the Quran's own speaking (25:52), as the life's total exertion (22:78), and as the defense with wealth and lives (61:11). The cause: fi sabilillah — the rescue of the crushed (4:75), the protection of every worship-house (22:40), the ending of persecution with the war's own termination clause (2:193). The forms: the inner struggle (29:69), the giving and the speaking, the permission of 22:39 and the command charter of 2:190-193 — 'do not transgress... if they cease.' The rule-book in full: treaties kept (9:4), the safety-seeker protected and escorted (9:6), the captive's rights (47:4, 8:70), peace-inclination answered (8:61), retaliation capped and the pardon priced better (16:126), no compulsion (2:256, 10:99). The ranks graded (4:95-96) and the transaction sealed (9:111: Allah has purchased the believers' lives and wealth — for them Paradise). The excuses answered verse by verse (9:38-39, 48:11, 9:90, 4:77) with the honest stayers' opposite file (Tabuk's three, 9:118). The testing's purpose (47:31, 3:142, 2:214), the help's source (47:7, 3:160, 40:51), and both modern thefts — the extremist's amputation of the ethics verses and the denier's airbrushing of the command — answered with a five-question reader's checklist. Closing with the reward: degrees, gardens, the living martyrs (3:169-171), and the double-pleasure of 9:100. Every verse cited in context; the tradition labeled as tradition; no ruling issued.
