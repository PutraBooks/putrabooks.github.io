Building Tomorrow's AI Engineers

S$1.90 · DRM-free EPUB + PDF

The machines got fluent. Someone still has to decide what they are worth. Every organization now has a machine that will answer any question it can be asked — in confident prose, at the price of electricity, with no sense of what it does not know. The AI engineer is the professional who stands between that fluency and the decisions it is about to drive, and the folklore about the job is wrong twice. The wizard myth says the work is oracle-hood: summoning intelligence from arithmetic, exempt from audit. The obituary myth says the work is glue: tuning mechanisms the machines have absorbed, awaiting the replacement date the forecasters keep moving up. Both miss the job that was always underneath: keeping the question. Building Tomorrow's AI Engineers is the twentieth volume in the Building Tomorrow's People series — the unflattered, practitioner-first account of the roles the machine era runs on. Written for the working engineer and the leader who funds the work, it installs the discipline as instruments: - The problem's excavation: interview the sponsor, write the hypothesis so it can be wrong, and price the scope against a practitioner's actual Monday - The data's quarry: read the sample as an argument, audit the labels' authorship, and find the seams nobody was told about before the postmortem does - The evaluation's instrument: baseline before model, metrics that earn decisions, slices, margins, and the held-out ceremony nobody rehearses on - The deployment's physics: the three drifts, thresholds owned and dated, and the claims your interface is making in your name - The failure's anatomy: the incident as the trade's real exam — rehearsals written in advance, the first hour's four moves, and the ledger repaired, not just the code - The room's evidence: handle the hype buyer without flattery and the audit as an ally rather than an obstacle - The operating program: the weekly hour, monthly afternoon, and quarterly day that turn the craft into a profession — four working days a year Skeptical? Good — this is a book about not believing things. It argues that the machines have made answers astonishingly cheap and honest questions more expensive than ever, and that the asset of this profession was never the mechanisms. It was the standing answerable — the one thing no successor model has ever proposed to take. Read honestly. Run by design.
