Hajj and Umrah: The Journey of Response

$0.99 · DRM-free EPUB + PDF

Hajj and Umrah: The Journey of Response walks the pilgrimage from Ibrahim's call to the flight home and the life after. The command and its condition (2:196, 3:97). The origins of every rite — Ibrahim's building, Hajar's run, the ransom of the sacrifice. Preparations before going: halal funds, settled debts, knowledge, and the taqwa that is the journey's true provision. The rites performed correctly: ihram, tawaf, sai, the standing at Arafah, the days of Mina, the sacrifice. The Umrah — the lesser journey. The return home — what the Quran says about going back. And the question every pilgrim family has asked: why do some people come back worse than before going? Seven Quranic diagnoses, and the maintenance plan that keeps the journey alive after landing. A closing chapter takes up the question of our time: the so-called DIY hajj — pilgrimage without authorization. Is it right? The Book's tests are applied one by one: the way-condition of 3:97 (a way refused by legitimate authority is not a way found — the duty is suspended, not performed by transgression), the authority-clause of 4:59, the covenant-command of 16:91, the unpaid-consumption file (2:188, 4:29 — every unpaid meal, ride, and room is wealth consumed without mutual consent), and the safety-clause of 2:195. Is such a hajj accepted? The evidence: 'Allah accepts only from the God-conscious' (5:27), 'the best provision is the taqwa' (2:197), and the tradition's conditional promise — a pilgrimage built on violations carries the very sins it came to erase. The door back is the Book's own: restitution of the debts, pardon of the authorities, sincere tawbah (66:8), and the lawful journey if Allah grants one (39:53, 65:2-3). A final chapter reads the dam-file — the expiatory penalties of the pilgrimage: the Book's three anchors (2:196's ransom-ladder of fasting, charity, or sacrifice; 5:95's hunting-equivalence judged by two just men; 22:37's taqwa-receipt), the scholars' classification (labeled): the tamattu and qiran offering, the ihram-prohibition violations (the fidyah-ladder), the infringed rites (the small-dam of a sheep), the missed Arafah and the intimacy-file (the greater-dam of a camel, with the hajj repeated), and the hunting-file's equivalence-scale — with the poor-pilgrim's substitute (the ten-day fast) and the graduated-mercy's lesson: the Owner never priced the poor out of the cure, and the dam itself never a license — 'the taqwa from you that reaches Him' (22:37) is the receipt the animal never replaces.
