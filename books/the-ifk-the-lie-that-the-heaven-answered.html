The Ifk: The Lie That the Heaven Answered

$0.99 · DRM-free EPUB + PDF

The Ifk — 'the monstrous lie' — is the event the Quran answered with a legislation-package: the slander of Aisha by a rumor launched in Madinah after the Banu al-Mustaliq expedition, and the ten-plus verses (24:11-26) that cleared her name, cursed the accusers, and wrote laws the world still needs. This book reads the whole passage and the event the tradition preserves around it (labeled as tradition throughout): the lie's social anatomy — 'a group among you,' manufactured by committee, carried by believers; the trial's paradoxical verdict — 'do not think it bad for you; rather it is good for you'; the witness law — four eyewitnesses or eighty lashes, the accusers 'liars with Allah' (24:4, 24:13), with the spousal li'an procedure (24:6-10) and the repentance's door (24:5); the rumor's physiology — 'you received it with your tongues... you thought it small while with Allah it was immense' (24:15); the believer's trained reflex — the good opinion and the glory-sentence (24:12, 24:16); the month of waiting — the Prophet's anguish and restraint, Aisha's own recorded account; the verdict from the Throne — the curse-verse (24:23), the limbs' testimony (24:24), and the pairing-verse's seal (24:26: 'those are innocent of what they say'); the spread-love's psychology (24:19) and the Shaytan's footsteps (24:21); the pardon clause — Abu Bakr's oath, Mistah's subsidy, and the forgiveness's economics (24:22); the afterlaw — verification (49:6), suspicion and backbiting (49:12), mockery and nicknames (49:11); and the whole passage as the information-age's founding charter, fourteen centuries early. The Quran's own restraint — the sinners' names declined, the acquittal without the amplification — is the book's first and last lesson.
