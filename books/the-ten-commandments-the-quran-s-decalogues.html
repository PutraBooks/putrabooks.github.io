The Ten Commandments: The Quran's Decalogues

$0.99 · DRM-free EPUB + PDF

The Quran contains its own ten-commandment passages — two of them, framed as complete recitations of the Lord's core demands. The first: 'Say: come, I will recite what your Lord has made sacred to you' (6:151-153) — ten commands in three verses, ending at 'this is My path, a straight one.' The second: 'Your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him' (17:23-39) — the Bani Israil-version, longer, ending at the shirk's return-warning. This book reads the ten command by command: the shirk-ban at both lists' head (the root the nine rest on, with Luqman's echo at 31:13); the parents (the uff-syllable banned, the wing lowered, the mercy-prayer returned); the children (poverty is no reason — the provision pledged inside the prohibition, and 16:58-59's era-grief as the backstory); the indecency-fence ('do not approach' — the border-verb's prevention-design, open and secret); the sacred soul ('except by right' — the court's careful exception, the heir bounded at 17:33, and 5:32's one-soul-as-all-mankind); the orphan's property (the improvement-exception, the maturity-handover of 4:6, and 4:10's Fire-verdict); the full measure (the even scale, 55:7-9's cosmic-balance, and the Mutaffifin's woe); the just witness (the relative's gravity, 4:135's whole-cost, and 17:36's three-organs — the hearing, the sight, the heart); the companion-version's extras (the pride-gait priced at the geology's scale, and the list's ring-structure); and the closing frames — the straight path of 6:153 and the tablets remembered at 7:145-154 (given, broken at the calf, restored at the mercy). The ten-count itself honestly labeled as the scholars' classical census, with the commands entirely the Book's own word. One Author, one core, one straight path — across the tablets and the two recitations.
